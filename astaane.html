01 — The problem

The biggest purchase of your life, made with the least information

The Iranian housing market is fragmented and opaque. Listings are spread across platforms like Divar, with no pricing standards and photos that often mislead. Most buyers end up relying on agents, who push their own inventory instead of what the buyer needs.

COVID-19 made it worse. Visiting homes in person became close to impossible, but buyers still had to commit their savings based on almost nothing reliable.

The pivot

We started with sellers and agents. After dozens of conversations, one thing was clear: buyers carried the most stress, took the most risk and had the least control. So we built for buyers.

02 — Research

Three kinds of buyers, three definitions of “the right home”

I ran in-depth interviews with more than a dozen buyers across life stages. Beyond what they wanted in a property, I looked at how they felt through the process and where their trust broke down.

InvestorsCare about ROI, rental yield and, above all, location.
Family buyersWant space, a good kitchen and a safe, family-friendly neighborhood.
Compact-living seekersWant short commutes, efficient layouts and affordability.

These personas shaped every decision that followed, from which information we surfaced first to the order of listing photos.

03 — Core challenges

Where trust broke down

1

No price transparency

Buyers couldn't tell whether a home was fairly priced, overpriced or a rare deal. They had to take the word of an agent with an obvious conflict of interest.

2

Broken discovery

Hundreds of listings at once, no relevance filtering and subjective copy. The result was decision fatigue, not better decisions.

3

Misaligned agent incentives

Agents promoted the listings they already had, not the ones that fit. Commissions were opaque and fraud was a real risk.

04 — Design goals

Three principles to design against

Build confidenceBuyers feel certain they're seeing the best options for their budget and criteria.
Reduce overwhelmFewer, more relevant listings instead of a flood of choices.
Make pricing legibleA clear, data-backed signal on whether a home is worth its asking price.
05 — Solution

Fewer homes, chosen for you, with honest prices

A · Personalized onboarding

We asked what matters in a home, not just budget and size

Onboarding asked which parts of a home mattered most (kitchen, natural light, bedroom size) and what kind of neighborhood felt right. We then reordered each listing's photos to lead with what that buyer cared about. A family buyer saw the kitchen first. An investor saw the location and floor plan.

B · One-at-a-time discovery

We borrowed the pacing of dating apps

Hinge, then the leader in meaningful matches, showed that a controlled one-at-a-time format beats a flood of options. We applied the same logic to homes: one card at a time, room to evaluate each before moving on. Liked homes go to viewing coordination. Passed homes are saved for later review.

Validated before we built itEarly tests with friends and family were consistent: one home at a time felt calmer, more focused and easier to act on.

C · Price transparency

One label: under, fair or over

We aimed for full market coverage in Janat Abad. We partnered with local listing aggregators and crawled Divar directly. From that data, each home got one label. No complex scores, just a reference point that didn't depend on the agent.

D · Verified listings

Every listing checked by hand

We called owners directly and asked for updated photos. Each photo was tagged by room type, so personalization could put the right room first.

07 — What we learned

Trust doesn't replace the need for control

The most valuable feedback came from going on real viewings with buyers. That's where we saw the emotional weight behind each decision.

“The app is great and easy to use, but I can't relax until I've searched everywhere myself. I'm spending my entire life's savings.”

Astaane pilot user, during a property visit
i
High stakes slow down behavior changeEven a clearly better product struggles to replace familiar habits when it's the biggest financial decision of someone's life.
ii
Nothing replaces observing users in contextInterviews told us what buyers said. Going on viewings showed us how they felt.
iii
Time-boxing creates clarityWe set a one-year limit from day one. When deeper traction needed more time and resources than we had, we shut it down on purpose.

Astaane was sunset after one year. That was a planned decision, not a failure. It still shapes how I approach research, trust-building and product scope.
